# Local SEO report: Noor Oriental Rugs (November 2026)

Checked on 2026-11-05, right after noororientalrugs.com moved to the new site. There are no search-volume or ranking numbers in this report because there is no Search Console data yet (see step 1). It is based on the site's own pages and on what competitors publish.

## Where the site stands

**Strong already**
- **One shop, one clear location.** 769 Concord Ave, Cambridge, MA 02138 and 617-868-6667 appear consistently, along with `LocalBusiness` structured data (address, geo, hours, phone, Facebook/Instagram/Yelp links), FAQ data on the homepage, and `Product` data on every rug.
- **Clean technical base.** Fast static pages (homepage HTML about 40 KB), HTTPS, one canonical domain, a sitemap of 52 addresses, robots.txt, and 54 permanent redirects from the old WordPress addresses, including the old articles, which now each go to their own new page.
- **Real reputation.** About 65 Yelp reviews averaging roughly 4.5 stars, plus a story competitors can't copy: a seventh-generation weaving family, Harvard-trained owner, charity work, rug donations.
- **Real blog** (new today): each article now has its own page and address, so each one can rank.

**Weak points, by impact**
1. **Service pages are thin.** Cleaning, restoration and appraisal pages have about 270–300 words each and one sub-heading. Competitors' equivalent pages run 400–1,100 words with several sections (process, signs you need it, pricing factors, pick-up area, FAQ). These are the pages that bring paying customers.
2. **The homepage title doesn't name the place.** "Noor Oriental Rugs | Fine Rug Repair & Restoration" leaves out Cambridge and Boston, which local searches lean on most. The H1 ("Museum-Quality Conservation and Master Craftsmanship") has no keyword.
3. **Rug pages are built by JavaScript** (`rug-detail.html?slug=…`). Google can render them, but more slowly and less reliably than plain HTML. 33 rugs = 33 chances to rank for searches like "antique Heriz rug Boston". Pre-building them as real pages, the way the blog now works, would fix this.
4. **No town pages.** People search "rug cleaning Newton", "Oriental rug repair Lexington", and so on. The homepage mentions Belmont, Lexington, Newton, Brookline, Wellesley and Concord, but no page is about any of them.
5. **The articles are short.** Eight of the ten migrated articles are 77–166 words. Google treats pages that short as thin. They're good material to expand.
6. **The footer has no name, address and phone.** Adding NAP and hours to every page's footer is a small, standard local-SEO signal.
7. **Owner decisions still open** (from the earlier audit): the appraisal "IRS / legally binding" wording, "three hundred years", the trade-in and in-home-trial badges. Unverifiable claims on a business site are a trust risk.
8. **The contact form isn't connected yet.** Not SEO, but every visitor SEO brings in who uses it is lost until Formspree is connected.

**Takeaway:** in Cambridge itself, Noor is the only dedicated Oriental-rug shop that shows up. Searches for Cambridge and the nearby western towns (Belmont, Arlington, Watertown, Lexington, Newton, Somerville) are the easiest to win. In Boston the competition is real, and the winners publish location-specific service pages and practical guides, which is the plan below.

## Keyword plan

Target one main phrase per page. Write it into the title, the H1 or first heading, the first paragraph, and the image alt text.

| Page | Main target | Supporting phrases |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Oriental rug store Cambridge MA | Persian rugs Cambridge, rug cleaning and repair Cambridge, rug shop near Harvard Square |

## Blog topics, in priority order

Each topic answers a real customer question and links to a service page. Mr. Noor's own photos and cases are what make them rank.

1. How often should you clean a Persian rug? (→ cleaning)
2. How much does Oriental rug cleaning cost in Boston, and what affects the price? (→ cleaning; only with prices the owner approves)
3. Moth damage in wool rugs: signs, prevention and repair (→ restoration)
4. Pet accidents on an Oriental rug: what to do and what not to do (→ cleaning)
5. Fringe repair vs. replacement: when to fix rug fringes (→ restoration)
6. How to tell a hand-knotted rug from a machine-made one (→ rugs for sale; expands "Authenticity")
7. What is my Oriental rug worth? How appraisals work (→ appraisals)
8. Donating a rug in Massachusetts: tax rules and Form 8283 (already exists; expand)
9. Heriz, Tabriz, Kashan, Kilim: a buyer's guide to Persian rug types (→ rugs for sale, link to matching rugs)
10. What size rug for a living room / dining room? (→ rugs for sale, size filters)
11. Spring and winter rug care in New England: salt, snow and dry heat (seasonal; local angle)
12. Before and after: restoring a [real customer rug] (case studies from the restoration gallery)

Aim for **two posts a month**. Expand the eight short articles to 600+ words first, because they already have addresses and redirects carrying old-site value.
